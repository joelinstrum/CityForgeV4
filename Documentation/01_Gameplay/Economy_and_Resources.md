# Economy and Resources

## The economic model

**CityForge has an economy, not an inventory.** The player sees construction and operating costs, jobs, prices, availability, wealth, and trade. Routine building should not require collecting fixed quantities of wood, stone, brick, or food icons. Resources matter because they help explain why a place develops as it does.

Local geography gives some economic capacity without a full production chain. Forests, fertile land, stone, clay, minerals, rivers, and harbors offer different opportunities. Industry improves or industrializes those opportunities. Lumberjacks and a sawmill can be visible in the world; the sawmill lowers lumber costs, creates jobs, may support exports, and changes the forest. The player should experience its effect without moving logs through a recipe tree.

**Industry accelerates opportunity rather than gating progress.** A town without a quarry can still build with masonry if imports are possible; it may pay more. A port city can prosper by importing materials, while an inland town with weak outside access may depend more heavily on local production. A profitable silver mine might finance imports despite limited local supply. Wealth does not erase geography: market access and transport change import prices and reliability.

An optional cost breakdown can explain a price: base construction, local supply, imports, labor, and transport. The headline price stays simple. Specific percentages, commodity lists, and formulas remain open.

## What CityForge is not

It is not an Anno-style chain where building B requires the player to produce and deliver a prescribed quantity of A. Economic systems should create choices and visible stories, such as a forest becoming an industrial district, rather than repetitive supply chores.

## Open questions

- Which resources need explicit simulation, and which can be folded into regional prices?
- How do trade access, transport cost, and economic shocks interact?
- How visible should production and movement be without becoming player-managed logistics?
