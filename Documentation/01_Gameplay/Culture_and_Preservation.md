# Culture and Preservation

## A city can value what it keeps

Culture should emerge from institutions, public space, architecture, civic life, and the identity built over time. Preservation should recognize the care given to significant buildings, neighborhoods, and landscapes. Legacy should express the accumulated long-term effect of these choices. These are related measures, not necessarily one score or a fixed formula.

Protection creates a planning decision. A protected building or cultural landscape can occupy valuable land, complicate a corridor, or limit redevelopment. Its value should not be reduced to a guaranteed Culture bonus that always outweighs the cost. Adaptive reuse, preservation, and new development could each be valid responses depending on the place.

Pre-existing cultural places deserve particular care. An Indigenous settlement, sacred area, historic farm, cemetery, or other culturally significant site may be present before the player's city. Such places should not be bulldozable rewards or generic obstacles. Any protective designation or relationship mechanic must reflect historical and regional context; a universal one-click solution would be misleading. The broader system should be moddable so different settings can represent different types of heritage responsibly.

Culture and preservation can influence Governor classifications and distinctions, but they should describe the city and the player's decisions rather than measure how many history notices were opened.

## Open questions

- How do protection, adaptation, and redevelopment affect land use and city identity?
- Who or what establishes a site's significance in different historical packages?
- Which preservation choices are permanent, reversible, or contested?
