# City Memory and Chronicle

## Places remember

Buildings, sites, streets, and neighborhoods should acquire records as the city changes. A building panel can show ordinary current information alongside its founding or construction date, former uses, protected status, and notable events. Events should be recorded when they occur so a later archive feels like the history of *this* playthrough.

**Illustrative example:** a church founded in the eighteenth century later hosts a local independence debate, is used during a conflict, hosts a civic meeting, serves as an emergency aid site, and is eventually designated a landmark. These are examples of the *kind* of record envisioned; dates, people, and event wording require historical review.

The Chronicle or newspaper combines three layers:

1. **Wider-world headlines:** accurately dated, curated historical context.
2. **Local reactions:** a national panic may affect or concern an industrial city, a port, and a small inland town differently.
3. **Procedural local news:** openings, infrastructure, noteworthy buildings, public events, and neighborhood change generated from the city's actual state.

Short headline-led editions may work better than long articles. The archive should let players revisit an event and find the associated place on the map. Important local events may also surface briefly when they happen.

## Editorial guardrails

Distinguish documented real people and events from fictional local names and procedural stories. Historical dates and language need validation. Do not fabricate a real person's actions or present procedural fiction as a documented fact. Sensitive subjects require content-specific review; human suffering must not become a reward loop.

## Open questions

- Which events become permanent records, and how are duplicates avoided?
- How much detail should the archive save over centuries?
- Should newspapers be periodic, event-driven, or both?
