CONTROLLED FINDINGS
Neutral Studio versus Current In-Game uses identical geometry, material source inputs, camera transform, projection and1600x1200 resolution. The production afternoon produces more pronounced facade relief, warmer stone and stronger roof/parapet shadows. The studio view exposes the shape without the same warm directional emphasis.

The one-variable series independently disables SSAO, removes the +.68EV exposure offset, or disables existing normal maps. State comparisons verify only the named control changes. Exposure has a much larger full-image effect than AO or the normal-map switch. The AO and normal-map whole-frame changes are below the unchanged temporal control in this run, so these captures do not establish a reliable quantitative ranking for those effects. Inspect local contact/detail regions and avoid treating small frame differences as an improvement score. TAA and dithering introduce small per-frame differences even when controls do not change; Temporal-Control-A and Temporal-Control-B quantify that floor. Pixel differences are diagnostic measurements, not realism scores.

The wall uses the supplied Weathered Reddish-Brownstone Wall image at1254x1254. Default importer power-of-two rescaling had reduced it to1024; the private v006 importer now retains its original dimensions. This wall has no authored normal map, metallic map or AO map. Its macro relief comes from geometry and light/shadow; turning up a missing normal map cannot add wall relief. The existing bay/cornice/source detail normal maps remain available and respond to the normal-map diagnostic.

The uncompressed wall comparison keeps the original image resolution, mipmaps, geometry, camera, lighting and other material values fixed. The uncompressed texture is a private lab copy. No broad loss of architectural detail is visible in the high-quality compressed close-up. Small pixel differences include TAA/dither variation; do not interpret the whole-frame difference as a precise compression-error score. Retain mipmaps for distant city views; disabling them may sharpen a still while worsening shimmer during camera movement.

The calibration chrome sphere visibly reflects the HDRI and neighboring building. Its response changes when reflection intensity is zero. The gray/white spheres and brick, sandstone, painted wood and glass swatches separate material response from the building's source textures. These are practical PBR comparison swatches, not a display-calibrated color chart. Absolute color accuracy cannot be established from an uncalibrated concept image and these renders alone.

COLOR SPACE INVESTIGATION
Two small isolated projects were used, not a production-project color-space toggle. Both use the same scene/assets, material properties, import settings, camera configuration, numerical light settings, volume profiles and URP renderer. One has Gamma; the other Linear. Matched Neutral Studio and Current In-Game views and control captures are included.

Gamma is markedly brighter and washed out under the existing Linear-calibrated numerical values. This isolates the color-space change; it is not a separately retuned Gamma art treatment. Keep production Linear. A future migration would need a whole-project review, not just a favorable building image.

Unity's Linear workflow samples color textures through the appropriate sRGB conversion and performs lighting in linear space. Non-color data such as normals, metallic and AO must retain data sampling rather than color conversion. Color-space changes affect blending, lighting response and existing material calibration; see the Unity documentation below.

Terrain: review layer blends, authored albedo/smoothness values, splat normalization, custom terrain shading, SH ambient fill and saved biome appearance. Existing district daylight calibration already has different Gamma and Linear branches; switching would change more than the GPU sampling mode in normal gameplay.
Vegetation: review leaf translucency, alpha-cutout shadows, billboard/near-to-far transitions, seasonal palettes and ambient tint. ForestFoliage.shader and ApprovedForestCanopy.shader contain explicit UNITY_COLORSPACE_GAMMA branches. These were inspected, not migrated or visually validated in the lab.
Water: review the existing Crest and custom water/refraction/reflection paths, depth-based tint, caustics, foam and blending. None of the production water scenes or shaders were modified or certified for Gamma by this investigation.
Other materials/shaders: inspect authored constant colors, texture color/data classification, emissive masks, roughness-to-smoothness packing and any manual gamma conversions. Revisit lightmaps/baked probes if the project setting changes. Existing building look is not a sufficient regression test for terrain, foliage, water or UI.

SAFETY AND RESTORATION
The new v006 subject assets live outside Resources under Assets/CityForge/Development/ArchitecturalRenderingLab/SubjectAssets/V006. The lab scene is excluded from player builds. An Editor build preprocessor restores the production pipeline and rejects an explicitly included lab scene. The existing v005 reference assets are reused read-only.

Private pipeline/renderer/feature and profile assets isolate controls from production assets. Scoped pipeline assignment is restored on scene change, assembly reload, quit, explicit stop and player-build preprocessing. A Library recovery record stores previous pipeline paths and serialized settings snapshots for unexpected interruption. Restoration rewrites only this lab's pipeline pointer fields, preserving other settings edits. The final verification compares original ProjectSettings, production rendering assets and pre-existing production scene hashes against their pre-task snapshots. Production color space stays Linear throughout the investigation.
