# CityForge Vision

## The promise

**CityForge is a city builder about place, time, and memory.** The player starts with a piece of geography, develops multiple settlements and districts, and watches those places change across generations. An early road can become Main Street; a working riverfront can become a park; a church or neighborhood can outlive the purpose for which it was built. The same map becomes more meaningful because the player remembers how it got that way.

**History passes through your city.** The world advances around it. The player does not invent steam engines or accelerate the calendar with research points. The year establishes what can exist; local geography, wealth, connectivity, density, institutions, and player choices influence when and how it appears in a particular city.

**The player becomes the steward and eventual curator of a place.** Early play emphasizes founding and development. Later, the city contains buildings, streets, neighborhoods, landscapes, and institutions with histories of their own. The choice to preserve, adapt, or replace something may matter as much as the choice to build it.

The world existed before the player arrived. A landscape may already contain communities, routes, culturally significant places, and traces of earlier activity. CityForge should treat those as part of the place, not as empty terrain awaiting ownership.

## The core activity

The player should spend most of their time **building, decorating, expanding, customizing, observing, and making occasional consequential decisions**. Systems below the surface can be rich, but they should ask for attention when there is an interesting choice, not impose continual upkeep. The city should visibly respond: forests thin, transport routes redirect growth, architecture changes, industries leave marks, and old places gain significance.

History is an ambient and optional layer. It should reward curiosity, not demand study. A player can enjoy the city builder without reading every newspaper, while another can spend hours exploring an archive of local events.

## What CityForge is not

CityForge is not a war game, an exhaustive history simulation, an inventory logistics game, or a campaign that forces one correct path through history. American history from roughly 1750 to the present is the default content frame, not a claim that every region shares one experience; other history and culture packages should be possible.

## Open questions

- How much agency should a player have over pre-existing cultural and historical places?
- What creates the best sense of consequence without turning the game into constant management?
- Which few moments in a city's life should receive special presentation?
