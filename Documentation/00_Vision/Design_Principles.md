# Design Principles

These principles guide tradeoffs when attractive features compete for time, complexity, or player attention.

1. **A place has a past before play begins.** Geography and existing human places shape what can be built. Do not present the map as historically blank.
2. **History passes through the city.** The calendar sets a credible world context. Each city adopts possibilities according to its own circumstances.
3. **The player authors a local story.** Roads, buildings, industries, public places, and preservation choices should leave legible traces over decades.
4. **History can be ignored without penalty for not reading it.** Ambient details, small prompts, and a deep optional archive form different levels of engagement.
5. **CityForge has an economy, not an inventory.** Local resources, labor, trade, and distance affect prices and opportunity; routine construction does not require managing piles of goods.
6. **Industry accelerates opportunity.** Sawmills, brickworks, mines, ports, and similar investments change costs, employment, wealth, and the landscape. They should not act as arbitrary recipe gates.
7. **Infrastructure is a civic decision, not a drawing chore.** Coverage, capacity, service quality, and cost matter. Pipe placement and repetitive maintenance are outside the intended focus.
8. **Geography and connectivity matter.** A harbor, river, road, canal, rail connection, or isolation changes which strategies are viable and affordable.
9. **Preservation has weight.** Culture, preservation, and legacy should register in the simulation and Governor portrait. Protecting a place can compete with development pressure without reducing that place to a simple point generator.
10. **Reward loops describe accomplishment and play style.** Numeric scores, titles, distinctions, and nearby goals should create curiosity. Descriptive titles may be admiring, neutral, critical, or humorous.
11. **Difficult history is handled with care.** Historical context may acknowledge human suffering; people are never commodities, production modifiers, or optimization rewards.
12. **Visual quality serves memory.** A beautiful living landscape makes change over time recognizable and worth caring about.

## Guardrails

Avoid Anno-style compulsory production chains, Civilization-style ahistorical tech racing, SimCity-style utility maintenance, and military conquest. Where a proposed mechanic adds chores, ask whether it instead can change prices, service quality, visible development, or a significant planning decision.
